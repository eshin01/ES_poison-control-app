It negotiates on interests, not on anger

Both BATNAs, stated

Patient: assistance application, payment plan on the undisputed part, federal dispute, regulator complaint. Hospital: sell the debt for a small share of face value, sue after 120 days, or write it off as charity care.

Objective criteria only

What Medicare pays for the same codes. The cash price and minimum negotiated rate in the hospital’s own transparency file. The 501(r) cap at amounts generally billed. Never a bluff, never a threat.

Expected value ranking

Each dispute is scored as dollars at stake times probability of success, adjusted for coverage. An in-network patient at the out-of-pocket maximum gains nothing from a facility coding fix, so the agent leads with EOB reconciliation and hardship instead.

Sequence that works: records and itemization, written dispute, assistance application, settlement at the anchor, escalation. Every offer in writing, every account on hold.

Options that expand value: lump sum for a paid-in-full letter, correct-and-pay, zero-interest plan, assistance first then settle the remainder.

This is Fisher and Ury applied to a billing office. The hospital’s interest is cash soon and low staff time. A prompt lump sum at a collections-level discount serves both sides.
