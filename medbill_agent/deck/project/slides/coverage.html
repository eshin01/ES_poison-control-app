Coverage decides the play. The agent picks the lever with the highest expected value.

Uninsured, nonprofit

Lead with the financial assistance application. Under 200% of poverty the policy itself says free. Corrections and settlement are the fallback if denied.

Uninsured, for-profit

No 501(r) duties. Lead with coding corrections, then the good faith estimate dispute, which freezes collections and anchors the bill to the estimate.

Insured, in network

The allowed amount is contractual and the out-of-pocket maximum binds, so facility coding fixes move the plan’s money, not the patient’s. Lead with EOB reconciliation, then hardship assistance.

Insured, out-of-network emergency

The No Surprises Act limits the patient to in-network cost sharing. One letter to compliance removes the balance bill; the coding fight belongs to the plan.

Every finding is scored as dollars at stake times probability of success, with the payoff recomputed through the patient’s actual deductible, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket maximum. The letter still lists every error; the strategy follows the money.

This is the slide that separates the agent from a bill-checking tool. Finding errors is table stakes. Knowing which errors are worth the patient’s time is the product.
